# Security checklist for an outside tester (MEDLINK)

Prepared in Phase 11 for a penetration tester hired by KHATIB TECHNICAL SOLUTIONS. Hand it over together with the repository, and with [`design/BACKEND-SPEC.md`](design/BACKEND-SPEC.md) for the intended rules.

## Rules of engagement
- **Test a staging copy, never production.** Use a separate VM with fictional data only. No real patient may ever be entered.
- **Accounts:**
  - the first owner account comes from `bootstrap-admin` on staging;
  - hospitals and staff are created from the console;
  - test passwords are generated by the tester and never sent over WhatsApp.
- **No denial-of-service** beyond checking that the rate limits answer 429. Do not run brute force for long against the Cloudflare-fronted hosts.
- **Report findings privately** to the owner, with steps to reproduce. Severity: critical, high, medium, low.

## Already checked automatically (every push, in CI)
- **100 API tests**, including:
  - every endpoint without signing in (401);
  - every role without the permission on every endpoint (403);
  - hospital isolation through the API and through PostgreSQL row-level security;
  - CSRF, lockout and TOTP replay;
  - Argon2id storage;
  - no secrets or personal data in the logs.
- **Backup cycle:** backup, monthly test, restore with the private key, and disaster recovery.
- **Dependencies:** `dotnet list package --vulnerable` and `npm audit`.
- **Secret scan:** gitleaks over the whole git history.
- **Phase 11, run by hand:**
  - OWASP ZAP baseline on the three sites;
  - an active scan of the booking site;
  - an API scan of the public and sign-in endpoints.

  ZAP found one bug (a NUL byte caused a server error), now fixed; the final API scan has 0 warnings.

## What to test

### 1. Sign-in and sessions (`/auth/*`)
- Lockout after 5 failures (15 minutes), and the per-IP and per-/24 limits. Do the limits survive a spoofed `X-Forwarded-For` or `CF-Connecting-IP` when the request does not come through Cloudflare?
- TOTP: replaying a used code, the time window, and enrolment through the one-time link.
- One-time links (setup, activation, reset): token guessing, reuse, expiry (30 min / 24 h / 1 h), and whether the link shows in the `Referer` or the logs.
- Session cookie `__Host-medlink_session` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict) and the `X-CSRF-Token` check on every state-changing request.
- Limits: 12 h idle and 7 days absolute; QR-scanner accounts 12 h absolute. Revocation on logout, role change, disable, suspension and password reset.
- Windows bearer tokens: theft from the PC (protected with DPAPI) and reuse from another machine.

### 2. Authorisation
- **Hospital isolation:** use IDs from hospital A while signed in to hospital B, on every `/hospital/*`, `/sync/*` and `/scan/*` route (bookings, users, devices, departments, closures, form versions).
- **Roles:** the matrix in the company console (Owner / Support / Viewer) and in the hospital (admin / reception / doctor / scanner):
  - doctors get their own departments only, without phone numbers;
  - scanners can check in only.
- **Company console:** last-Owner protection; Support's limited audit view; recovering a hospital admin requires the three identity checks.

### 3. Patient booking site and QR (`/public/*`, `/scan/*`)
- Enumerating booking numbers or hospitals, and guessing QR codes (128-bit, stored as a hash). The scanner lookup is limited to 60 per minute per IP.
- Two patients taking the same seat at once; replaying the `Idempotency-Key`; booking outside the window or on closed days; per-phone limits.
- The answers a form accepts (types, lengths, required fields) against the published version. Old forms are accepted for 2 hours after a publish.
- What another hospital's QR code reveals at a scanner (it should reveal nothing).

### 4. Injection and display
- **Stored XSS:** patient names, form questions and answers, department names and cancellation reasons are shown in:
  - the hospital web;
  - the company console;
  - the Windows app (WPF);
  - the printed or saved ticket.
- **SQL injection:** all SQL is parameterised (Dapper). Check the dynamic parts, e.g. the audit filter.
- The Content-Security-Policy in `web/*/public/_headers`: try to bypass it. `style-src` allows inline styles (needed by React); scripts come from the site only.

### 5. Windows app and sync
- The local SQLCipher database: key stored with DPAPI under `%LOCALAPPDATA%\MEDLINK`; copying it to another PC or user.
- Sync push and pull: forged events, events for another hospital, implausible times, a revoked device, more than 25 devices.
- Phase 12 adds the installer and Ed25519-signed updates; test update tampering then.

### 6. Server and infrastructure
- The firewall accepts 80/443 from Cloudflare only and SSH from the owner's IP. PostgreSQL is not reachable from outside.
- TLS (Caddy, Let's Encrypt), HSTS, the 1 MB body limit. The API container is read-only and runs without privileges.
- `.env` permissions (600); no secret in images, the repo or the logs.
- Backups: the R2 token's reach (it can delete); the private key is never on the server.

### 7. People and process
- Password recovery over WhatsApp must never change a password by itself. Support verifies the identity, then a one-time email link is sent. Try social engineering against the written procedure.
- Hospital admins' email accounts: a reset link goes to the email address, so the security of that mailbox matters.

## Known accepted risks (owner decisions)
- One free VM with no failover. Backups are the safety net (data loss of up to 6 hours).
- Inline styles are allowed by the CSP.
- The web pages load fonts from Google Fonts (see the Phase 11 questions).
- The device security of hospital PCs and phones is the hospitals' responsibility.
